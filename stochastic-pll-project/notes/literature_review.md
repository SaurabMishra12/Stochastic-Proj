# Verified literature and the scope of the proposed extensions

Source verification date: 13 November 2026. This is a targeted, source-checked review for the project, not an exhaustive systematic review or a claim to cover every development through 2026. Bibliographic records are stored in `report/references.bib`. Publisher pages, author-uploaded arXiv papers, the ACL Anthology and the official dataset repository were used as primary sources. No numerical results in these sources are presented as results of the present simulations.

## SDE model and numerical methods

**Kloeden and Platen (1992), *Numerical Solution of Stochastic Differential Equations*.** [Publisher and DOI](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-12616-5). The supplied p.274 was inspected directly, establishing the PLL equation (10.4), its negative additive-noise term, `a=RC`, the amplitude-product interpretation of b, and cycle slips as `2pi` changes. Springer verifies the authors, title, 1992 copyright, series volume 23 and DOI. This is the model source. The supplied paragraph does not independently fix a covariance normalization for white noise; the project declares a unit-standard-Wiener convention. We do not attribute a physical seconds calibration or a global PLL capture theorem to this page.

**Higham (2001), “An Algorithmic Introduction to Numerical Simulation of Stochastic Differential Equations”, *SIAM Review* 43(3), 525–546.** [Publisher and DOI](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/full/10.1137/S0036144500378302). The publisher's accessible article covers stochastic integration, Euler–Maruyama, Milstein, strong/weak convergence and stability. It provides the numerical vocabulary for distinguishing pathwise coupled error from weak differences in expectations. In our constant-diffusion model, the derivative of diffusion is zero, so the standard Milstein correction vanishes by direct algebra. A claimed Milstein improvement would therefore be incorrect. The project's convergence rate is measured from its own coupled meshes and finite numerical reference; it is not copied from a generic theorem without checking its hypotheses and resolved errors.

## PLL slips and nonlinear capture

**Bobrovsky and Schuss (1982), “A Singular Perturbation Method for the Computation of the Mean First Passage Time in a Nonlinear Filter”, *SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics* 42(1), 174–187.** [Publisher and DOI](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0142014). The publisher's abstract identifies second-order PLL cycle slipping with a Kolmogorov exit problem and describes a singular-perturbation expression for mean interslip time. This supports treating slips as nonlinear first-passage phenomena rather than Gaussian deviations alone. Only the abstract and bibliographic record were available here; the specific asymptotic formula and hypotheses were not independently checked in the full article. Accordingly, the project does not substitute that formula for simulated waiting-time estimates or claim to reproduce the paper. Its core-and-dwell detector is an explicitly stated operational approximation, with right censoring and detector sensitivity reported.

**Bianchi, Kuznetsov, Leonov, Yuldashev and Yuldashev (2015), “Limitations of PLL simulation: hidden oscillations in MatLab and SPICE”, ICUMT, 79–84.** [Author paper and verified journal-reference metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02484); [conference DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICUMT.2015.7382409). The full author manuscript illustrates how solver settings and initial states can hide oscillatory behavior in a two-phase PLL with a lead–lag filter. Its model is not the same as the present textbook equation. Its relevance is methodological: selected stable-looking trajectories cannot prove a global capture range, and local equilibrium stability is weaker than global acquisition. We use this distinction in interpretation and validate timesteps; we do not claim that these authors proved a hidden attractor in our baseline model or that our finite simulations eliminate all such possibilities.

## Escape and stochastic stability

**Kramers (1940), “Brownian motion in a field of force and the diffusion model of chemical reactions”, *Physica* 7(4), 284–304.** [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0031-8914(40)90098-2); [primary-article scan hosted at MIT](https://www.mit.edu/~kardar/research/seminars/translocation/Kramers1940.pdf). The original article studies Brownian escape from a potential well, with damping-dependent regimes. It provides the historical basis for interpreting a tilted potential and noise-assisted barrier crossing. Our barrier heights are derived directly from `U(phi)=-Delta phi-ab cos(phi)`. These heights motivate near-threshold sampling, but they are not an exact slip-rate formula. The study does not claim an Arrhenius prefactor, an asymptotic rare-event law, or an exponential waiting-time distribution merely from a small number of observed transitions. Full-text access was available from the scan; the publisher fetch was blocked, while publisher search metadata confirmed the DOI and pages.

**Khasminskii (2012), *Stochastic Stability of Differential Equations*, second edition, Springer, volume 66.** [Publisher and DOI](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-23280-0). The publisher verifies the edition, author, 2012 copyright and coverage of moment/almost-sure stability, ergodic properties and stationary solutions. The electronic publication date is in 2011; the citation uses the publisher's copyright year 2012. This is a theoretical reference for distinguishing notions of stochastic stability. Publisher metadata and the contents were verified, not every theorem. In the present additive-noise SDE, the deterministic equilibrium is not absorbing because diffusion remains nonzero there. A finite-horizon capture proportion is therefore not a proof of almost-sure asymptotic stability at a point. That conclusion follows directly from the model and is not attributed to an unchecked theorem number.

## Synthetic phase-oscillator networks

**Acebrón, Bonilla, Pérez Vicente, Ritort and Spigler (2005), “The Kuramoto model: A simple paradigm for synchronization phenomena”, *Reviews of Modern Physics* 77, 137.** [Publisher and DOI](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.77.137). APS verifies the authors, title, year and article starting page; its abstract describes coupled phase oscillators, mathematical analysis, numerical methods and model variants. It motivates using an explicit oscillator network as a separate synthetic extension. The circular order parameter `R=|N^-1 sum exp(i theta_j)|` summarizes phase concentration, not answer accuracy. Our topology, noise-correlation, dropout and delay experiments are project-defined variants, not claimed reproductions of this review. The publisher abstract and bibliographic record were accessed; detailed review claims beyond that accessible scope are not needed for the experiment interpretation.

## LLM communication: empirical connection, not a physical derivation

**Li, Du, Zhang, Hou, Grabowski, Li and Ie (2024), “Improving Multi-Agent Debate with Sparse Communication Topology”, Findings of EMNLP, 7281–7294.** [ACL Anthology and DOI](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.427/). The official record reports GPT/Mistral experiments in which sparse debate communication can attain comparable or better performance while reducing computational cost. This provides a directly relevant empirical baseline for fixed sparse communication. It does not establish that language-model reasoning obeys a PLL SDE, that semantic embeddings have a physical phase, or that synchronization maximizes correctness. Any proposed stochastic controller must therefore beat independent, full-communication, fixed-sparse and disagreement-triggered comparisons under matched prompts, roles, models and call budgets where feasible. The verified record and abstract support these limited contextual claims; this project does not assert a comprehensive 2026 state of the art or reproduce the published numerical gains.

**Wang, Li, Huang and Dong (2026), “Do We Need Complex Topology Control? Distinct-Peer Random Routing Improves Cost-Efficiency in Sparse Multi-Agent Debate”.** [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27150). The arXiv record verifies submission on 22 September 2026 and explicitly labels the work a preprint. Its abstract reports a strong random-without-replacement distinct-peer routing baseline and lightweight stopping. We use this as current, abstract-level evidence for testing simple communication and stopping policies before attributing gains to controller complexity. We do not claim peer-reviewed confirmation, reproduce its numerical results, or assume that its peer-count setting transfers unchanged to a three-agent system. This source strengthens the baseline requirements; it does not supply a physical phase interpretation.

**Cobbe et al. (2021), “Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems”.** [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168); [official GSM8K repository](https://github.com/openai/grade-school-math). The paper introduces GSM8K and studies verification of generated mathematical solutions. The official repository provides JSONL questions and answer-bearing solutions with final answers marked by `####`. A future coordination evaluation can use the official test split with labels kept out of all model-visible prompts, and a separate development subset for tuning thresholds. Dataset membership, row count, hashes and version must be recorded from downloaded files before execution. The original paper's results concern its stated models and protocol; they are neither a current accuracy baseline nor evidence that a phase-based controller works. No benchmark accuracy or API result is available from the local PLL simulations.

## Literature-informed LLM decision

The local stochastic study can establish how specified mathematical systems behave under controlled simulated noise, detuning and network communication. It cannot itself validate a mapping from language-model messages to circular phase. A defensible next LLM experiment would first specify the representation and test whether it predicts subsequent answer disagreement, revision or correctness beyond ordinary semantic/answer disagreement. Choose thresholds on development data and freeze them before evaluating the complete official test split. Compare the same agent models and role prompts; log logical evaluations separately from HTTP attempts/retries, latency and token usage. Match calls or state unavoidable compute differences. Retain all failures and benchmark exclusions.

The proposed LLM extension therefore remains a research protocol unless those representation, budget, credential and approval gates are satisfied. These gates are scientific and authorization requirements from the user specification; they are not evidence of completed LLM experiments. No claim of a novel physics-derived LLM communication law is supported by the verified sources or by a synthetic oscillator network alone.
